Where were Australia's birds recorded?

Queensland recorded the widest variety.

THE TAKEAWAY

Recorded variety differs from reporting activity.

Queensland recorded 359 distinct species in April 2025. New South Wales recorded 344, despite having more than twice as many observation records. This difference matters: submitted sightings reflect where people reported birds, not a complete survey of every species living in each state.

Which birds were recorded most often?

THE TAKEAWAY

Australian Magpies lead the records.

The Australian Magpie appeared in 2,567 observation records, compared with 1,752 for the second-ranked species. That is a difference of 815 records. These counts describe reporting frequency in this dataset, so a higher position does not necessarily mean a larger wild population.

How are bird body lengths distributed?

THE TAKEAWAY

Shorter body lengths are more common.

Of 745 species with usable body-length measurements, 562 were 40 centimetres long or shorter. That is about three quarters of the measured species. A smaller number extend into much longer length ranges, creating a distribution with a long tail to the right.

Observations concentrate near settled coastal areas.

The grid map shows more submitted records across parts of eastern and southern Australia than in many inland areas. Each coloured cell combines reports from one geographic grid location. Which days attracted more bird reports?

THE TAKEAWAY

Reporting activity changes from day to day.

On 9 April, observers submitted 3,808 records, compared with 1,891 on 24 April. The darker cells mark dates with more reports. Differences may reflect survey effort, participation or weather, so this calendar does not demonstrate that bird populations changed during the month.

What do historical conservation records show?

Compare historical conservation categories and their recorded changes.

Most entries were classified Least Concern.

THE TAKEAWAY

Least Concern is the largest classification.

The 2015 reference lists 629 species-level entries under Least Concern. Smaller groups include 41 Near Threatened entries and 34 Vulnerable entries. This chart describes historical classification counts, not the number of birds or their present-day conservation status.

How did selected categories change over time?

THE TAKEAWAY

Some historical classification counts increased.

Critically Endangered entries rose from 8 in 1990 to 13 in 2015. Endangered entries increased from 22 to 33 across those same assessment years. These changes describe how the reference classified species at different times; they do not directly measure changes in wild bird populations.
